01 / STRUCTURE

Track what depends on what

Represent task requirements as predicates and prerequisite relations, preserving independent branches.

02 / PROGRESS

Distinguish progress from repetition

Persistent states track verification, invalidation, and repair. Repeating established progress earns no new credit.

03 / CREDIT

Turn graph changes into rewards

Dependency-weighted potential assigns positive, negative, or zero step credit for the learner.

01 — THE IDEA

A failed episode can still contain useful work.

Terminal rewards tell an agent whether it succeeded, but offer little guidance about which steps mattered. Work built on an unresolved mistake should receive less credit, while independent progress can remain valid.

DEPENDENCY-AWARE CREDIT

Judge a step by how it changes valid task progress—not simply by where it appears in a trajectory.

DARS represents progress as a dependency graph. An annotator identifies which predicates a step verifies, invalidates, or repairs. A fixed graph potential converts those events into signed rewards.

02 — THE METHOD

Structured annotations. Deterministic credit.

01

Represent requirements

Build a graph of predicates and prerequisites. Establishing a parent does not automatically verify a child.

02

Replay persistent states

Track verification (V), invalidation (B), and repair (P). Repair lifts a break; re-verification establishes the predicate again.

03

Measure net progress

Discount verified descendants of broken prerequisites, sum their contributions, and reward the change in potential.

r̃t = ρ · clip(ΦG(t) − ΦG(t−1), −κ, κ)

The graph potential averages dependency-weighted verified predicates. The annotation interface supports GiGPO, ARPO/AEPO, and tool-free reasoning integrations. Clipping and learner-specific transformations mean the deployed signal has no general policy-invariance guarantee.

05 — WHAT THE ABLATIONS SHOW

Credit placement and graph structure both matter.

Step-level credit preserves useful information

With the same annotations, restoring step credit raises tool-free averages from 56.90 to 58.23 at 1.7B and from 79.13 to 79.50 at 4B. The 4B difference is not statistically resolved.

Independence belongs in the graph

Replacing WebShop’s requirement graph with a trajectory-ordered chain lowers success by 14.2 points and task score by 18.4 in the topology ablation.

Attenuation is better than blanket suppression

In a separate ALFWorld sweep, moderate attenuation reaches 94.3% success, versus 90.2% with none and 84.2% with hard masking.

A smaller annotator can be sufficient

A distilled Qwen3-8B annotator reaches 92.2% peak ALFWorld success versus 89.1% for its API teacher, within decoding noise.

Ablations use their own experimental settings and should not be read as additional rows of the headline comparison.
